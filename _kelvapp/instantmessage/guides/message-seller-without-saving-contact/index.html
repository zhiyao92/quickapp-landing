Message a seller without adding another contact to your iPhone

Use InstantMessage for a one-off chat: check the number, choose a supported service, prepare a clear message and review before sending.

By Kelvapp · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

Your address book probably does not need another entry called “Chair person,” “Plumber maybe” or “Delivery Tuesday.” Sometimes you need one conversation, not a permanent place in Contacts.

InstantMessage helps prepare a chat without saving the recipient first. You enter the number, choose a supported messaging service and continue there. That last step matters: the messaging service handles the conversation, not a separate InstantMessage network.

Start with a number you meant to use

Take the number from the seller or service provider’s intended contact details. Check that it belongs to the person or business you want to reach, especially if you copied it from an old conversation or a forwarded screenshot.

Select the right country and calling code in InstantMessage, then review the complete number. Do not let a familiar default country slip into an international conversation unnoticed. Clipboard detection can offer to paste a number, but an offered paste is not proof that the number is current or correct.

Choose a service the other person uses

Pick the relevant messaging option available on your device. InstantMessage supports handoff to services including WhatsApp, Telegram, Viber, Line and SMS, but that does not mean every service can open every phone number.

The installed app, the recipient’s account and the kind of identifier the service accepts all matter. If the seller gave a specific contact method, follow that rather than guessing. A service may need an ID or another supported route instead of a number.

For a straightforward number-based chat, use an option that supports that number and check the destination when it opens. If a handoff fails, confirm the service and details before trying again.

Write a first message with enough context

A useful opening tells the recipient what you are asking about and gives them one clear question. “Hi” followed by a long wait is technically a conversation starter, but it makes both people do extra work.

For a listing, you could write: “Hi, I’m asking about the desk you listed. Is it still available, and would Saturday afternoon work for collection?” For a service enquiry, name the service and ask about availability before sending unnecessary personal details.

Those examples are starting points. Change the item, timing and question to match what you actually want. Do not promise a purchase or a pickup time unless you mean it.

Use a template without sending yesterday’s details

If you often send similar enquiries, use a preset reply or create a custom template. Enable Save Custom Messages if you want to maintain your own reusable library.

Keep the reusable wording general. Before each handoff, replace the item name, date and any other specific details. Read the entire message, including the bit you are certain you already changed. Templates save typing; they do not know that this week’s seller is offering a bicycle rather than a bookshelf.

Review again in the messaging app

  1. Check the displayed recipient against the original contact information.
  2. Review the prepared text and any changes made during handoff.
  3. Remove details that the recipient does not need.
  4. Send only when the destination and message are correct.

The chosen service’s account, sending and privacy rules apply. Opening a conversation without saving a contact is not anonymous messaging. The service may show your profile or number according to its own settings.

Keep the scope small

You do not need to clean up your entire address book to send one message. InstantMessage’s contact-review tools are a separate workflow; review any proposed changes before confirming them.

For a one-off exchange, the goal is simply to reach the right person with a clear question and skip an unnecessary saved contact. Keep payment information, verification codes and sensitive documents out of a casual first enquiry. A convenient chat launcher cannot verify a seller or guarantee that a transaction is safe.
